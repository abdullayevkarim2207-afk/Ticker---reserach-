## Initial thesis and evidence

Ford’s Ford Pro segment is a consequential operating driver because it produced $6.843B of FY2025
Segment EBIT on $66.286B of external revenue. However, that result does not by itself establish
common-equity value. The company reported FY2025 special items of $17.356B, including a $8.1B
Model e asset impairment and a $3.2B impairment related to the expected BlueOval SK JV
disposition. These items require explicit normalization analysis rather than mechanical use of
reported earnings in a forecast. Ford also consolidates Ford Credit, whose $141.417B of debt is
linked to a financing business with finance receivables and leased assets; the appropriate
enterprise-value architecture and debt treatment must be established before a per-share conclusion.

Sources: 2025 Form 10-K, Note 25 (segment results and special-item description) and Note 18
(debt).

## Consequential assumptions

1. **Valuation object:** Value automotive operations and Ford Credit in a manner consistent with
   the selected FCFF / enterprise-value architecture. Do not subtract Ford Credit debt without
   also addressing the related finance receivables and leasing assets.
2. **Cash treatment:** Do not automatically add all $23.356B of consolidated cash as excess
   cash. Determine the operating and regulatory liquidity needs of Ford and Ford Credit first.
3. **Earnings normalization:** Treat the FY2025 special items as a named normalization question,
   not as recurring operating performance by default.
4. **Share basis:** Use a stated diluted-share convention. FY2025 GAAP diluted shares equal basic
   shares because the loss made potential shares anti-dilutive; a positive-earnings forecast may
   require a different diluted-share calculation.
