## Business description and initial thesis

Ford is a global automotive company that earns revenue from the sale of internal-combustion, hybrid, and electric vehicles; commercial vehicles and related services; and financing through Ford Credit. Its reportable segments include Ford Blue, Ford Model e, Ford Pro, Ford Next, and Ford Credit.

The strongest current operating business is Ford Pro, which serves commercial and government fleet customers with trucks, vans, fleet services, telematics, and charging solutions. In FY2024, Ford Pro revenue increased 15% to $66.906B and Segment EBIT increased to $9.015B, producing a 13.5% EBIT margin. The filing attributes the revenue growth to higher wholesales, favorable mix, and higher net pricing.

The largest offset is Ford Model e. FY2024 Model e revenue fell 35% to about $3.9B and its Segment EBIT loss was $5.076B. Ford attributes the weaker result principally to lower pricing in a competitive EV market and lower wholesales. This does not make Ford Model e valueless, but it makes the path to EV profitability a load-bearing forecast assumption.

Ford's consolidated revenue and net income increased in 2024, while Company adjusted EBIT declined slightly from $10.416B in 2023 to $10.208B in 2024. The difference between GAAP operating income and adjusted EBIT must be reconciled before using either as a sustainable base-year earnings measure.

## Risks and falsification tests

Ford identifies intense competition and pricing pressure, warranty and recall costs, supplier and supply-chain disruption, economic conditions, and tariffs as material risks. These risks can reduce production, increase costs, or weaken vehicle demand.

**Falsification question:** What evidence would show that Ford Pro's FY2024 Segment EBIT is not durable enough to support a normalized forecast?

**Tests to run:**

1. Separate Ford Pro volume, pricing, mix, warranty, and service-revenue drivers in Item 7 and the segment note.
2. Compare Ford Pro EBIT margin with a downside case that reduces fleet demand and pricing while increasing warranty and material costs.
3. Identify whether any reported Ford Pro earnings depend on one-time items or intersegment pricing.
4. Test whether Model e losses, EV pricing pressure, or tariffs create funding demands that weaken cash available to the automotive business.

## Enterprise-to-equity bridge control

Do not calculate a Ford per-share value from the reported balance-sheet figures yet. Consolidated cash is $22.935B, but $9.272B belongs to Ford Credit; Company-excluding-Ford-Credit cash is $13.663B. Consolidated debt is also not a simple common-equity claim: Company debt excluding Ford Credit is $20.654B, while Ford Credit debt is $137.868B and is related to a financing business with receivables and leased assets.

The cash-flow statement reports $23.190B of cash, cash equivalents, and restricted cash at year-end, compared with $22.935B of balance-sheet cash and cash equivalents. The $0.255B difference is restricted cash. This is a reconciled definition difference, not a number to average.

Only then classify cash, debt, finance receivables, leases, noncontrolling interests, and pension obligations consistently.
