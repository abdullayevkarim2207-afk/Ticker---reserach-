# Lab 08 — Ford peer P/E and DCF comparison

All prices and per-share amounts are U.S. dollars. The comparison date is **September 10, 2026**, the valuation date used in my Week 3 Ford DCF work.

## My peer-selection policy

For a company to qualify as a peer for Ford, I think it should mainly be in the automotive business and make money by manufacturing and selling vehicles. It would also be better if it has a financing or leasing business similar to Ford Credit. I am okay with some differences like company size, where they operate, types of cars they sell, or how much they focus on EVs, but I would mention these differences. I would exclude a company if most of its business is outside the auto industry, its business model is too different from Ford, or it has negative earnings because then the P/E comparison would not really work.

I wrote this policy in my own words. I applied it consistently and did not change it simply to obtain a preferred valuation result.

Evidence that would make me reject a candidate includes: manufacturing not being its primary business; earnings coming mainly from a structurally different activity; annual reported diluted EPS being nonpositive; an unreconciled ADR, currency, or share-class basis; earnings published after September 10, 2026; or a price that cannot be matched to the comparison date.

## Define and discover

**Focused question:** For Ford Motor Company, what reported P/E evidence is supplied by profitable listed vehicle manufacturers that share Ford's capital-intensive manufacturing, parts/services, and captive-finance economics as of September 10, 2026, and can that evidence support an implied Ford share value beside my Week 3 DCF range of $12.39–$19.71?

Ford earns revenue by selling internal-combustion, hybrid, and electric vehicles; selling commercial vehicles and services through Ford Pro; and providing retail, lease, and dealer financing through Ford Credit. Ford's [2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 and Note 25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37996/000003799626000015/f-20251231.htm) is the primary source for this business and segment description.

Ford's latest annual reported earnings public by the comparison date were negative. Its fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, and its February 10, 2026 [annual earnings release](https://s205.q4cdn.com/882619693/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Ford-Q4-2025-Earnings-Press-Release.pdf) reported a full-year net loss of about $8.2 billion. The [2025 Form 10-K, Consolidated Income Statements and EPS table](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37996/000003799626000015/f-20251231.htm) reports a loss available to common shareholders of $8.182 billion and diluted EPS of **-$2.06**. Therefore, a reported trailing P/E cannot support a Ford share value. Multiplying a peer multiple by negative EPS would create an economically meaningless negative price, not a valuation result.

What still required research at the discovery stage was the candidates' exact business mix; annual GAAP diluted EPS and publication dates; date-matched prices; share and currency bases; and whether the finance businesses were sufficiently similar. A later lab could investigate normalized or forward earnings, but this lab does not switch away from annual reported diluted EPS.

## Skeptical AI review and source judgment

**Skeptical criticism:** The weakest supported DCF assumption is the $3.5 billion starting Company-adjusted free-cash-flow proxy: it is non-GAAP and excludes Ford Credit, while the proposed P/E denominator is consolidated GAAP diluted EPS. The methods therefore differ not only in forecasting versus market pricing but also in valuation perimeter and earnings definition. There is also a small price-timestamp mismatch between the $13.90 intraday reverse-DCF target and the $13.88 closing-price comparison.

**Question that could change the decision:** If Ford reports positive full-year GAAP diluted EPS, what evidence would show that both the automotive cash flow and Ford Credit earnings are durable enough to compare with GM and PACCAR rather than merely reflecting reversal of 2025 special charges?

**My answer:** Even if Ford returns to positive earnings, I would want to see strong and consistent free cash flow before changing my decision because one profitable year alone would not be enough to show that the improvement is sustainable.

**My source check and judgment:** I accept this criticism because my DCF does not include Ford Credit, but Ford's reported earnings do, so the two methods are not valuing exactly the same business. The DCF also depends a lot on long-term assumptions, and Ford currently has negative earnings. Because of these issues, I am not confident enough to invest yet, so I would still choose **watch–defer**.

## Reflection and conditional call

GM is the stronger fit because it combines broad passenger and truck manufacturing with services and captive finance. PACCAR adds useful evidence about commercial vehicles, parts, and finance, but I qualify it because its commercial-only mix and profit structure differ from Ford. The investigation shows that profitable vehicle manufacturers can trade at positive reported P/E multiples, but it adds no defensible Ford share-price estimate while Ford's denominator is negative.

My provisional decision is **watch–defer** because the DCF shows some upside, but the evidence is not strong enough for me to invest yet. The evidence that could most easily change my mind is Ford returning to positive earnings while keeping strong free cash flow.

I can defend the Week 3 DCF sensitivity range of **$12.39–$19.71**, subject to its cash-flow and valuation-perimeter limitations, but I withhold a peer-P/E range because Ford's FY2025 reported diluted EPS is negative.
